Strengthening Fiscal Sustainability: Global Debt & Deficit Analysis

Government debt is growing at an unsustainable pace across dozens of countries, and the data makes clear that without structural reform, the consequences will be severe. This policy brief and interactive Tableau dashboard analyze global trends in government spending, revenue, and public debt from 2010 to 2023, drawing on datasets from the IMF, World Bank, and OECD. I designed comparative visualizations including debt trend lines, cross-country scatterplots, and bubble charts to translate complex macroeconomic data into accessible insights, culminating in evidence-based policy recommendations around fiscal rules, revenue mobilization, and transparent debt management. This project pushed me to think critically about how data storytelling can make dense policy topics legible to a broader audience, which is something I would continue to prioritize in future work.

02

Impacts of Gun Characteristics on the Lethality of Gun Violence in the United States

The type of firearm and how it was obtained both have measurable effects on how deadly a gun violence incident becomes. This statistical analysis examines how firearm characteristics influence casualty rates in U.S. gun violence incidents using a comprehensive 2017 dataset, investigating whether long versus short barrel firearms and stolen versus legally possessed status were associated with statistically significant differences in casualties per incident. Through comparative bar charts and proportion analysis, we found that long guns and legally possessed firearms were associated with higher casualty proportions. My role focused on structuring the research questions, interpreting results, and designing visualizations that made sensitive findings understandable without oversimplification. Working on this project reinforced how important it is to handle difficult data with both analytical rigor and ethical care.
